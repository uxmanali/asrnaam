Arabic boy names beginning with H

The English letter H has no single Arabic counterpart. The 67 names on this page open with 2 different Arabic letters, and in the Science of Letters each of those carries a different quality, element and divine name. Two names that both look like H in English can begin very differently once they are written in Arabic.

  • حHa opens 50 of them, among them Hamza, Huzaifa, Hamdan.
  • هHa (light) opens 16 of them, among them Hashim, Hisham, Haroon.

Questions about Muslim names starting with H

How many Muslim boy names start with H?
AsrNaam lists 67 of them. Across boys and girls together the letter H accounts for 131 researched names, 67 for boys and 64 for girls. Every one has its Arabic spelling, its meaning, and its letter-by-letter Ilm ul Huroof reading.
What Arabic letter does H correspond to?
There is no single answer, which is what makes this letter interesting. Most often H stands for Ha (ح), which opens 50 of these names. It can also stand for Ha (light). Each of those Arabic letters has its own element and divine name in the Science of Letters, so two names that look alike in English can read very differently.
Which Muslim boy name starting with H is most popular?
By what visitors actually search for and open on this site, Hashim, Hamza and Huzaifa lead the letter H. The settled meaning of Hashim is: The crusher, the generous ancestor of the Prophet who fed the pilgrims. Popularity is worth knowing, but the tradition weighs meaning first.
Are there Quranic names beginning with H?
Yes. 4 of the names on this page appear in the Quran or belong to a figure named in it, among them Hamza, Hud and Husain. A Quranic name is not required for a name to be Islamic, but many parents look for one.
What is the Ilm ul Huroof reading of a name starting with H?
Open any name on this page and you will find it: the name broken into its Arabic letters, each letter with its element, its abjad value and the divine name it is associated with, then a reading of the whole. It is a tradition for reflection, not for prediction.
