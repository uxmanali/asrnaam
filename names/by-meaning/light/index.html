What Muslim names meaning light have in common

Arabic separates two kinds of light, and Muslim naming keeps the distinction. Nur is light that is received: the lamp lit from elsewhere, the light of guidance. Diya is light a thing produces of itself, the burning of the sun. The Quran uses both of the sun and moon in the same verse, giving the sun its diya and the moon its nur. Names in this family sit on one side or the other, and it changes what the name is saying.

The classical roots this meaning is carried by:

  • ن-و-رnur, light, the light that is given rather than made.
  • ض-و-أdaw, the light a thing gives off by itself.
  • ش-ر-قsharq, to rise, as the sun rises, hence Ishraq.
  • ز-ه-رzahr, to shine and to flower, the same root for both.

What is the Arabic word for light?
Nur, from the root ن-و-ر: light, the light that is given rather than made. Arabic carries more than one word for it, and the others are set out above, because they do not mean the same thing.
