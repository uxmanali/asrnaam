What Muslim names meaning blessed have in common

Barakah is the central word, and its root also gives baraka, a camel kneeling to rest. The image is of a good that settles rather than passes through: not a large amount, but an amount that lasts and increases where it is. Saad is fortune, the turn of circumstance. Yumn is blessing tied to the right side, which is why Ayman means both right-handed and blessed.

The classical roots this meaning is carried by:

  • ب-ر-كbarakah, blessing as increase that settles and stays.
  • س-ع-دsaad, good fortune, felicity.
  • ي-م-نyumn, blessing associated with the right hand.

What is the Arabic word for blessed?
Barakah, from the root ب-ر-ك: blessing as increase that settles and stays. Arabic carries more than one word for it, and the others are set out above, because they do not mean the same thing.
