What Muslim names meaning gift have in common

Wahb is a gift with nothing expected back, which is why al-Wahhab is a divine name and why so many children long awaited are named from this root. Fadl is different again: surplus, what exceeds the due. The tradition uses it for grace, the good that arrives beyond anything earned. Ata is the plain act of handing something over. A child named from any of these is being called a gift, not an achievement.

The classical roots this meaning is carried by:

  • و-ه-بwahb, to give freely, without return, hence Hiba.
  • ع-ط-وata, to hand over, a gift placed in the hand.
  • ف-ض-لfadl, bounty, what is given beyond what is owed.
