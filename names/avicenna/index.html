Who Ibn Sina was

Avicenna is not a name anyone was ever given. It is what medieval Latin Europe made of Ibn Sina, the Persian physician and philosopher who died in 1037, and it stuck so hard that most English-language medicine remembers the Latin and not the Arabic.

His Canon of Medicine, al-Qanun fi al-Tibb, was a standard teaching text in European universities into the seventeenth century, which is roughly six hundred years of continuous use. His metaphysics, particularly the distinction between essence and existence, shaped Aquinas and, through him, a great deal of what Europe went on to think.

Sina is used as a given name in Iran and Turkey. Avicenna is not a given name in any tradition, and this page exists to connect the two halves of one man's reputation.

Questions about Avicenna and Ibn Sina

Is Avicenna the same name as Ibn Sina?
Avicenna is the Latinised form and Ibn Sina is the Arabic form of the same name. They refer to the same man.
Is Avicenna the same person as Ibn Sina?
Yes. Avicenna is the Latinised name European scholars gave to Ibn Sina.
Is Avicenna used as a first name?
No. It is a Latinisation, not a name given to children. Sina is used as a given name in Iran and Turkey.
Can a Muslim family use the name Ibn Sina?
Ibn Sina is a family name rather than a given name, so it is not usually given to a child. Its page on AsrNaam sets out the man and the meaning of the name.
