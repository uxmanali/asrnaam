Is Qurrat-ul-Ain an Islamic name? Yes. Qurrat-ul-Ain is an established name in Muslim use. Its settled meaning is: Coolness of the eyes; comfort of the heart. It was carried by Qurratulain Hyder.. Islamic tradition asks that a name carry a good meaning and claim nothing that belongs to God alone, and this name meets both.

Qurrat-ul-Ain Name Meaning in Islam

A reading of Qurrat-ul-Ain: the meaning, the letters, the demand it makes on the one who carries it.

Section 1 · Arabic Root

Where the Name Comes From

ق-ر-ر

ق-ر-ر (qarra, to be cool, to rest) + عين (eye). This root is the linguistic-spiritual family of the name; other names sharing the same root carry a related weight.

Qaf holds the divine axis, Ra moves with mercy, Ta manifests, then Ain (the eye itself) opens, Ya reaches, Nun closes into hidden treasure. The name literally has "ain" (eye) at its centre.

Section 3 · Synthesis

What This Reading Says About You

Qurrat-ul-Ain's letters draw from many elements at once, a full palette, not a single note. This is a name that asks its bearer to hold contradictions gracefully: warmth and steadiness, movement and depth. Running through the reading is Al-Qadir, the All-Capable, carried by a person as a capable, unruffled competence. A person named Qurrat-ul-Ain carries a full weather system inside a single quiet chest.

Section 4 · What Families Say

What People Usually Mean by This Name

قرۃ العین Qurrat-ul-Ain کا مطلب: آنکھوں کی ٹھنڈک، دل کی راحت، ایسی جس کے دیکھنے سے آنکھوں کو ٹھنڈک اور دل کو سکون ملے۔

Qurrat-ul-Ain, a Quranic phrase, literally means "the coolness of the eyes" – a classical Arabic idiom for "the source of joy, the delight of the heart." The phrase appears in the Quran when the family of the believers is described as their "coolness of the eyes" (Furqan 25:74). As a girls' name, Qurrat-ul-Ain is one of the most poetic in the Urdu-Arabic tradition, given by parents who see their daughter as a literal consolation for their eyes and heart.

Section 5 · Bearers

Who Has Carried This Name

  • Qurratulain Hyder. 1927–2007. Legendary Urdu novelist and short-story writer.
  • Tahereh (Qurrat-ul-Ain). 1814–1852. Persian poet and religious reformer.
