Abdul: Servant of Whom?

Abdul is one of the most common name elements in the Muslim world and one of the least complete. It means servant of the, and the sentence stops there until a name of God finishes it. Below is the rule, the reason the spelling shifts to Abdur and Abdus, and every full form AsrNaam carries.

What does Abdul mean? Abdul is not a name by itself. It is عبد, servant, joined to ال, the, and it waits for the divine attribute that follows: Abdul Rahman, servant of the All-Merciful. Read alone it means only servant of the, with the of left open.

What Abdul actually says

The root is ع-ب-د, to serve and to worship, and the two senses are the same act in Arabic. عَبْد is the one who does it. Attach the article and you get عبد ال, servant of the, which is a phrase holding its breath. Every Abdul name is that breath completed. Abdul Aziz is the servant of the Mighty. Abdul Hakim is the servant of the Wise. The person is not being named after a quality; he is being named in relation to the One who holds it.

This is why the second word carries the meaning and the first carries the posture. Two boys named Abdul Rahman and Abdul Qadir are not sharing a name. They share a stance, and are pointed at different attributes of God from it.

Outside the Arabic-speaking world, forms and passports have done what forms do. A registrar hears two words, writes the first in the given-name box and the second in the middle-name box, and a generation later Abdul is living as a full first name in Bradford, Marseille and Detroit. Nothing is wrong with the man who carries it. It is worth knowing, though, that the name is asking a question the second word was meant to answer.

Why it becomes Abdur, Abdus, Abdun

Arabic sorts its letters into sun letters and moon letters, and the article ال behaves differently in front of each. Before a moon letter the ل is pronounced, so عبد العزيز is read Abdul Aziz. Before one of the fourteen sun letters the ل falls silent and the letter after it doubles instead, so عبد الرحمن is read Abdur Rahman, never Abdul Rahman, however it is spelled on the certificate.

The written Arabic never changes. Only the reading does. Both romanisations you will see in family documents are therefore defensible, and neither is a mistake.

Questions about the name Abdul

Is Abdul a complete name on its own?
Not in Arabic. Abdul is عبد ال, servant of the, and the definite article is left hanging. The name is finished by the divine attribute that follows it: Abdul Rahman, servant of the All-Merciful; Abdul Aziz, servant of the Mighty. Registrars in Britain, Europe and North America often record only the first half, which is how Abdul came to be used as a standalone given name outside the Arab world.
What does Abdul mean in Urdu?
عبد ال, the servant of. In Urdu the compound is usually written and spoken whole, as عبد الرحمن or عبد الحمید, and the two words are read as one name. The meaning sits in the second half.
Why is it Abdur Rahman and not Abdul Rahman?
Because of the sun letters. When the article ال is followed by one of fourteen letters, the ل stops being pronounced and the following letter doubles instead. Rahman begins with ر, a sun letter, so عبد الرحمن is read Abd-ur-Rahman. Aziz begins with ع, a moon letter, so the ل is kept and it stays Abdul Aziz. Both spellings of the written name are correct; the difference is in how it is said.
Is it wrong to name a child Abdul by itself?
Scholars generally hold that عبد should be joined to a name of God rather than left alone or joined to anything else, which is why compounds such as Abdul Nabi are discouraged: servitude in this construction belongs to God. A child already registered as Abdul is not in difficulty. The usual advice is simply to use the full compound when calling him.
How many Abdul names are there?
Ninety-nine in principle, one for each of the names of God, though not all are used in practice. AsrNaam carries 94 full forms with the Arabic, the meaning and the Ilm ul Huroof reading for each.
