How names work in Turkey and the Turkic world

Turkish naming runs on two tracks. One is Arabic and Persian, arriving with Islam and adapted to Turkish sound: Mehmet from Muhammad, Yusuf kept as it is. The other is Turkic and much older, names built from the language itself, where Ay is moon, Deniz is sea and Han is a ruler. Aynur, moon-light, joins one to the other.

Questions about Turkish Muslim names

How many Turkish Muslim names are there?
AsrNaam lists 142 used in Turkey and the Turkic world, 60 for boys and 82 for girls. Naming traditions overlap rather than divide, so many of these are used well beyond the region too.
Are Turkish names Arabic names?
Not all of them, and the distinction matters. A name is Islamic through its meaning, not through the language it comes from. Some of the names here are Arabic and travelled; others come from the languages of Turkey and the Turkic world and were carried into Muslim naming locally. Both are on this page, each with its origin stated.
Which Turkish Muslim name is most popular?
By what visitors search for and open on this site, Hazal, Nihal and Kenan lead. The settled meaning of Hazal is: Hazel, autumn colour.
Do these names have an Urdu spelling?
Yes. Every name on this site carries its Arabic spelling and its Urdu spelling separately, because Urdu writes ک ی ہ where Arabic writes ك ي ه. You can also browse the whole library by Urdu letter.
