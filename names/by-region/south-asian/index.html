How names work in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh

South Asian Muslim naming draws on three streams at once: Arabic for the religious core, Persian for the courtly and poetic register that the Mughals brought, and the local languages for everything else. A name like Gulzar is Persian, Ayesha is Arabic, and both are entirely at home in Lahore. The Urdu spelling matters here and is given separately on every name page, because Urdu writes ک ی ہ where Arabic writes ك ي ه.

Questions about South Asian Muslim names

How many South Asian Muslim names are there?
AsrNaam lists 173 used in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, 69 for boys and 104 for girls. Naming traditions overlap rather than divide, so many of these are used well beyond the region too.
Are South Asian names Arabic names?
Not all of them, and the distinction matters. A name is Islamic through its meaning, not through the language it comes from. Some of the names here are Arabic and travelled; others come from the languages of Pakistan, India and Bangladesh and were carried into Muslim naming locally. Both are on this page, each with its origin stated.
Which South Asian Muslim name is most popular?
By what visitors search for and open on this site, Aisha, Zaviyar and Ayra lead. The settled meaning of Aisha is: Living, lively, prosperous, Mother of the Believers, wife of the Prophet.
Do these names have an Urdu spelling?
Yes. Every name on this site carries its Arabic spelling and its Urdu spelling separately, because Urdu writes ک ی ہ where Arabic writes ك ي ه. You can also browse the whole library by Urdu letter.
