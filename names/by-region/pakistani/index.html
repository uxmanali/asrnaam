What makes a name Pakistani

Pakistani naming sits on three languages at once. Arabic supplies the root and therefore the meaning, Persian supplies most of the imagery that Urdu poetry runs on, and Urdu supplies the spelling and the sound a family actually uses. A name here can be Arabic in the lexicon, Persian in feeling, and Pakistani in every practical sense.

This page is deliberately narrower than the South Asian region page. A name appears here only where the research note on its own entry records Pakistani use, which is a smaller and more honest claim than assigning every South Asian name to a country. If a name you expect is missing, it is missing because the evidence was not recorded, not because it is not used.

The Urdu spelling matters more than English-language name sites usually allow. Two names that look identical in Latin letters can be written differently in Urdu and mean different things, and the Urdu form is the one that will appear on a birth certificate, a school register and a passport.

Questions people ask

What are the most popular Pakistani Muslim baby names?
AsrNaam does not rank them, and the honest reason is that reliable Pakistani birth-registration data is not public at the granularity a ranking would need. The 87 names here are listed alphabetically with the research behind each one, so you can judge rather than trust a made-up chart.
Do Pakistani names have to be Arabic?
No. Islamic tradition asks that a name carry a good meaning and not claim for a person what belongs to God alone. Persian, Turkic and Urdu names meet both tests routinely, and a great many of the best-loved Pakistani names are not Arabic in origin at all.
Why does the Urdu spelling differ from the Arabic?
Urdu adds letters Arabic does not have and reshapes several it does. The same name is often written with ہ where Arabic uses ة, or with ی where Arabic uses ى. Both are correct in their own script, and every entry here gives both.
What is the lucky number on each page?
It is the Abjad value of the name, the numeric weight the classical Science of Letters assigns to its Arabic letters. It is a feature of the tradition, not a prediction, and the Ilm ul Huroof page explains exactly what it is and is not.
