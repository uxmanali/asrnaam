How names work in Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei

The largest Muslim population on earth names its children in a script that is now Latin, from an Arabic vocabulary, in languages unrelated to Arabic. The result is distinctive: the -ah ending is favoured for girls, Nur and Siti are everywhere, and Sanskrit names that predate Islam in the archipelago sit comfortably alongside Arabic ones.

Questions about Indonesian Muslim names

How many Indonesian Muslim names are there?
AsrNaam lists 44 used in Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei, 15 for boys and 29 for girls. Naming traditions overlap rather than divide, so many of these are used well beyond the region too.
Are Indonesian names Arabic names?
Not all of them, and the distinction matters. A name is Islamic through its meaning, not through the language it comes from. Some of the names here are Arabic and travelled; others come from the languages of Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei and were carried into Muslim naming locally. Both are on this page, each with its origin stated.
Which Indonesian Muslim name is most popular?
By what visitors search for and open on this site, Muhammad, Aulia and Nurhidayah lead. The settled meaning of Muhammad is: The most praised.
Do these names have an Urdu spelling?
Yes. Every name on this site carries its Arabic spelling and its Urdu spelling separately, because Urdu writes ک ی ہ where Arabic writes ك ي ه. You can also browse the whole library by Urdu letter.
