Is Binta an Islamic name? Yes. Binta is an established name in Muslim use, Arabic in origin. Its settled meaning is: Daughter. Islamic tradition asks that a name carry a good meaning and claim nothing that belongs to God alone, and this name meets both.

Meaning

Binta (بنتة) means: Daughter. West African; from Arabic bint.

Section 1 · Arabic Root

Where the Name Comes From

ب-ن-ت

The root letters of Binta are ب-ن-ت. From this same root the classical Arabic dictionaries derive a whole family of words that share the underlying idea. In Ilm ul Huroof each letter of the root is read for its ontological quality; the sum of the root letters gives the field of meanings that the name is set inside.

See the letter reading above for how each letter carries its share of the whole.

The letters of Binta unpack as follows in the classical Science of Letters:

بنتة

Ba (ب): the house, containment, the vessel of meaning  ·  Nun (ن): the fish, knowledge, the inkwell  ·  Ta (ت): the sign, the mark left, evidence  ·  Ta marbuta (ة): the closing, the feminine seal

Section 3 · Synthesis

What This Reading Says About You

Binta is grounded in earth, the element of stability, patience, and quiet holding. The letters ask their bearer to be reliable rather than showy, to keep the household of the heart in order. Stitched through this reading is Al-Batin, the Interior, meaning the person carries an interior life that runs deeper than what is shown. A person named Binta builds ground under other people's lives.

Section 4 · What Families Say

What People Usually Mean by This Name

Binta is an Arabic name, drawn from the classical Arabic lexicographic tradition (Lisan al-Arab, al-Sihah, Taj al-Arus). The name carries the meaning: Daughter. West African; from Arabic bint.

Every letter of Binta in the Arabic script has been read in the classical tradition of Ilm ul Huroof as an ontological principle: the letters do not merely spell the sound of the name, they carry the qualities the tradition ascribes to it. See the letter reading above.

In Islam, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: "You will be called on the Day of Resurrection by your names and the names of your fathers, so give yourselves good names" (Abu Dawud). A well-chosen name is a lifelong invocation of the meaning it carries.
